# Berlin Papyrus 3027 source evidence review

Status: research evidence only. This report does not approve a passage, populate `functions/sourcePassages.json`, or authorize a Firestore write.

## Edition and access

The inspected edition is Adolf Erman, *Zaubersprüche für Mutter und Kind: aus dem Papyrus 3027 des Berliner Museums* (Berlin: Königliche Akademie der Wissenschaften, 1901). The Internet Archive item includes the full scan and OCR:

- <https://archive.org/details/b24866003>

Erman's introduction identifies the manuscript as Berlin Papyrus 3027, describes its physical extent, and explains that it contains protective and healing texts concerning mothers and children. The edition supplies hieroglyphic transcription, German translation, manuscript coordinates, and commentary.

The 1901 edition predates the current United States public-domain cutoff. Erman died in 1937, placing the text beyond a life-plus-70 term as well. Final rights approval must still record the application's distribution jurisdictions and must not assume rights in later translations.

## Inspected passages

### Seven-knot morning/evening protection

- Edition pages: 40–41.
- Manuscript locator: Papyrus 3027 reverse 2,7–3,3.
- Attested features: a protective text associated with sunrise; a seal and hand made into a protective object; seven knots; one knot in the morning and another in the evening until seven are complete.
- Unsafe or unsuitable context: the object is attached to a child and the text frames danger through hostile dead or disease.
- Proposed adaptation: **Seven-Knot Household Guardian** retains seven knots and alternating morning/evening construction, but places the finished cord out of reach as a household marker tied to observable safety actions. It is never worn or placed on a child.

### Four-thread, four-knot textile construction

- Edition page: 37.
- Attested features: textile preparation described with uncertain verbs translated by Erman as spinning or twisting; four linen threads; four knots; placement on a child.
- Editorial uncertainty: Erman expressly marks parts of the textile vocabulary and damaged text as conjectural. A lesson may retain the counts and general cord-making action but must label braiding as a modern choice.
- Proposed adaptation: **Four-Strand Working Cord** retains four strands and four knots as a tabletop sequence marker, removes bodily placement, and adds storage and entanglement precautions.
