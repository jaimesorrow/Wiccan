Inside the book

What you will find.

Home

The moon has turned.

The night’s moon and the Wheel of the Year stand at the front of the book. Each practice tells you when to work: the full moon, a waxing crescent, a quiet evening by lamplight.

Daily lesson

One page, and no other.

No endless feed, no noise. Each day one spell, potion, or lesson rises to meet you, with its intent, its materials, its steps in order, and the words to speak when the moment comes.

Grimoire

A library that grows with every moon.

Nothing you receive is lost. Every rite gathers in your own Grimoire, by date or by craft: candle spells, cord and knot work, charms, crystals, and potions.

The working

The steps, written on the page.

The daily lesson sets the working out as text: the steps in order, and the words you may speak when you come to them.

Materials

Named on the lesson.

Herbs, crystals, candles, and vessels are listed on the lesson itself, so you can set out what the working asks for before you begin.

The Wheel

The season, on Home.

Home shows the Wheel of the Year and the sabbat at hand, with the moon the practice is written under.

Roots you can trace

The old source, named beneath the work.

Wiccan's rites are drawn from historical texts such as the London-Leiden magical papyrus and the Metternich Stela. Where a source is used, the work and passage are named, so you may read the ancient words for yourself and see how each modern practice was shaped from them.

What sets Wiccan apart: real historical sources, cited where available, behind every practice.
